## Through the lens

### A fake job interview opened the way to a $6 billion theft

Binance says the attackers compromised three engineers' computers over several months. In June one engineer installed a tampered Homebrew package found while searching for an AI coding tool; in September a second was lured through fake job interviews into running a malicious coding "assignment"; from there the attackers reached a third engineer's machine and the wallet signing service. The exchange says roughly $2.85 billion has been frozen or intercepted. **Connects to:** [Unit 2: Thinking like an attacker: threat modeling, risk, and the industry](../units/threat-modeling.html). You'll meet this in unit 2: a threat model asks which people and machines can sign transactions, and this attack went after the humans with that access. ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/rigged-coding-assignment-abused-ai-tool-opened-path-to-6-billion-binance-heist/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/hackers-steal-6-3-billion-from-binance-in-record-crypto-heist/))

### A Linux kernel bug chained into root-level web shells

CISA lists a kernel flaw where a missing variable initialisation lets attackers alter data held in the page cache. Its entry says the bug can be chained with two other flaws to plant PHP webshells and run commands as root. **Connects to:** [Unit 1: Your lab, and Linux through a security lens](../units/lab-and-linux.html). This is the current unit's theme in the wild: the permission model is enforced by the kernel, so a kernel bug can bypass it. Patching and least privilege both matter. ([CISA KEV catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog))

### TV boxes that came infected from the factory

Google says Videostrong, a TV box maker, shipped devices with malware that rented out the owners' connections as residential proxies and sold access to other criminals. The takedown targeted the domains and servers controlling them. **Connects to:** [Unit 3: Networks from an attacker's view](../units/networks-and-exposure.html). You'll meet this in unit 3: cheap internet-connected devices with no update path are a standing part of what defenders have to account for on a network. ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/tech-firms-dismantle-botnet-that-turned-1-3m-tv-boxes-into-proxies/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-dismantles-botnet-secretly-running-on-1-3-million-android-tv-boxes/))
